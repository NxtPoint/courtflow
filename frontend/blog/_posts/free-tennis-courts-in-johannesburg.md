"Free tennis courts near me" is one of the most-searched tennis phrases in Johannesburg, and almost nothing written about it is honest.

So here is the honest version. Genuinely free, genuinely playable public tennis courts are rare in Johannesburg — rarer than in most cities of comparable size. That does not mean you have to join an expensive club to play. It means the cheap options are different from the ones you are probably searching for, and knowing which is which saves you a wasted Saturday driving to a court with no net.

## The short answer

There is no reliable network of free, maintained, floodlit public tennis courts across Johannesburg. What exists instead falls into four categories, and only one of them is genuinely free:

- **Municipal and park courts** — occasionally free, highly variable in condition, rarely floodlit, almost never bookable.
- **School and university courts** — usually good surfaces, but access is restricted to pupils, students or hired groups.
- **Complex, estate and hotel courts** — free if you live there, inaccessible if you don't.
- **Pay-as-you-go clubs** — not free, but bookable, maintained, floodlit, and cheaper per session than most people assume.

Most people searching for a free court actually want the fourth thing: **a decent court they can definitely play on, at a price that doesn't require a membership.** That option exists and it is not expensive.

## Why "free" often isn't free

The genuinely free courts in Johannesburg come with costs that don't appear on a price list:

**They're rarely bookable.** No booking means no certainty. You drive out, someone is already on court, and you either wait or go home. Two wasted trips cost more in petrol and time than an hour of court hire.

**Condition varies enormously.** A free court can mean a good surface with a taut net, or it can mean cracked tarmac, no net and no lines. There is usually no way to know before you arrive, because nobody is maintaining a listing.

**Almost none are floodlit.** This is the big one in Johannesburg. Without lights you are playing before work or on weekend mornings only — and in summer, the late afternoon thunderstorm takes out a good share of those too. Floodlights are what turn tennis from a weekend intention into a weekly habit.

**No partner, no coach, no structure.** A free court solves the venue problem and nothing else. If you don't already have someone to play with, an empty court isn't the thing you were missing.

None of that means free courts are worthless — if you have a regular partner, a car and a flexible morning, they're a perfectly good option. It means "free" answers a narrower question than most people are asking.
