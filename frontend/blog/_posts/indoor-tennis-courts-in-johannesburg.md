"Indoor tennis courts near me" is a search a lot of Johannesburg players make in winter, or on the third grey afternoon in a row. The honest answer is short: **there is almost no public indoor tennis in Johannesburg**, and once you understand why, you probably won't want it anyway.

## The short answer

There are a handful of covered or indoor courts in Gauteng, and nearly all of them sit inside private schools, private estates or academies that don't take public bookings. There is no equivalent of the indoor centres you'd find in London or Munich, where you book a court online for an hour in January.

If you're searching for indoor tennis in Joburg, you are almost certainly going to end up playing outdoors. The good news is that in this city, that's genuinely fine.

## Why Johannesburg never built them

Indoor tennis exists where the weather makes outdoor tennis impossible for months at a time. Johannesburg's weather does not.

- **Winter is dry and sunny.** June and July are our coldest months, but they are also our clearest. A Highveld winter afternoon is often 18-20°C and windless — close to perfect tennis weather.
- **The rain is summer rain, and it's brief.** Joburg storms arrive in the late afternoon, empty themselves in 40 minutes and leave. They rarely take out a whole day.
- **We get roughly 2,500 hours of sunshine a year.** Building and heating an indoor centre for a city like this has never made commercial sense.

The result: Johannesburg solved the same problem a different way — **with floodlights**.

## Floodlights are what you're actually looking for

For most people, "indoor" is shorthand for two real needs: *I want to play after work when it's dark*, and *I don't want the weather to cancel my game*.

Floodlit courts solve the first outright and most of the second. An evening court in Joburg in midwinter is dark by 18:00 — but under lights that makes no difference at all. And in summer, an afternoon storm usually clears in time for a 19:00 booking.

At **NextPoint Tennis** in Killarney we have **seven floodlit hard courts, plus Gauteng's only clay court**, at Killarney Country Club in Houghton Estate. You can book any of them online in under a minute, and evening play under lights is the busiest part of our week for exactly this reason.

## What to do when the weather does interfere

A few practical habits from players who never lose a week:

1. **Book the evening slot, not the afternoon one.** Summer storms are an afternoon phenomenon. A 19:00 court is far less likely to be rained off than a 16:00 one.
2. **Play on hard courts right after rain.** Hard courts drain fast and are usually playable within half an hour of a storm passing. Clay takes longer.
3. **Don't book weeks ahead in storm season.** Book two or three days out, when the forecast is actually meaningful.
4. **Have a standing partner, not a standing booking.** It's easier to move a game than to cancel one.

## What it costs to play outdoors here

Because there's no indoor premium to pay, Johannesburg tennis is cheap by international standards.

- **Courts are pay-as-you-go from R90**, and you don't need a membership to book one.
- If you play often, **membership from R220 a month** covers unlimited court bookings.
- **Lessons and classes are pay-as-you-go**, booked separately from court hire.

A few, almost all attached to private schools, estates or academies. None operate as a public book-online-by-the-hour indoor centre.

**Can I play tennis in Johannesburg in winter?**
Yes — winter is one of the best times to play here. Days are dry and sunny, and floodlit courts cover the early sunset.

**What happens if it rains on my booking?**
Hard courts drain quickly and are often playable within 30 minutes. Get in touch if a storm takes out your slot and we'll sort it out.
